A. The framed film

Recommended

Why it works

  • The headline sits on the light page, so it always reads, whatever frame the film is on.
  • The film bleeds off the right edge and stands on the threshold line; a panel slides away to start it, like a door opening.
  • Keeps the site light, so the craft section stays the one dark moment.
  • On phones the copy and both buttons come first; the film runs edge to edge just below.

Trade-offs

  • Less immersive than a full-screen film.
  • The square crop trims the sides of the wide room shot (the door itself stays in view).

B. The full-bleed film

Why it works

  • Closest to the current site: the film fills the whole first screen.
  • The full room shot shows at its natural width.
  • Copy sits on a solid Ink panel, so contrast holds on every frame.

Trade-offs

  • Adds a second dark area to a light site (the brief allows one).
  • The nav has to stay solid from the start, so it cannot be transparent over the hero.
  • The text panel covers part of the film on phones.

Recommendation

Go with A. It keeps the film you asked for while protecting legibility and the light, architectural feel, and it keeps the door-opening reveal as the first impression.

Both respect reduced motion and Data Saver: the film then shows its first frame with a play button instead of autoplaying.
